## What Exactly is "1 Unit" of Electricity?

Before touching any formulas, you need to understand what you are being billed for.

In electrical engineering and utility billing:

> **1 Unit of Electricity = 1 Kilowatt-hour (kWh) = 1,000 Watt-hours**

This means if you consume **1,000 watts of electrical power continuously for 1 hour**, your meter logs exactly **1 unit**.

### Quick Everyday Real-World Equivalents of 1 Unit:
* A **10-watt LED bulb** running for 100 hours (10W x 100h = 1,000 Wh).
* A **75-watt ceiling fan** spinning at top speed for 13.3 hours (75W x 13.3h ≈ 1,000 Wh).
* A **1.5-ton 5-star inverter AC** (averaging ~900 watts running load) running for ~1.1 hours.
* A **2,000-watt immersion rod or geyser** running for just 30 minutes (2,000W x 0.5h = 1,000 Wh).

## The Step-by-Step Formula: How Electricity Bills are Calculated

Your total monthly bill consists of **four primary components**:

> **Total Payable Bill = Energy (Slab) Charges + Fixed Demand Charges + Fuel Surcharge (FAC / FPPCA) + Electricity Duty / Tax**

Let's break down each step:

### Step 1: Find Units Consumed from Your Meter Reading
Digital meters display cumulative units consumed since installation. You need two numbers:
1. **Current Month Reading:** Note the reading with the `kWh` indicator on your meter screen.
2. **Previous Month Reading:** Found on last month's paid bill.

> **Units Consumed = Current Reading - Previous Reading**

*Example:* If your meter read `04320 kWh` on August 1st and reads `04640 kWh` on September 1st:
* **Units Consumed = 4,640 - 4,320 = 320 units**

---

### Step 2: The "Telescopic Slab" Magic (Why Higher Tiers Don't Penalize Everything)

The biggest misconception in electricity billing is assuming: *"If my bill entered the ₹8/unit slab, will all my units be billed at ₹8?"*

**No!** Indian DISCOMs follow a **telescopic slab structure**. Your consumption is sliced into brackets, and each bracket is billed at its respective rate.

#### Typical Domestic Slab Example:
* **First 100 units (0 to 100):** ₹3.50 / unit
* **Next 200 units (101 to 300):** ₹5.80 / unit
* **Next 200 units (301 to 500):** ₹7.50 / unit
* **Above 500 units:** ₹9.20 / unit

If you consume **250 units**, your energy charge is:
* First 100 units x ₹3.50 = **₹350**
* Remaining 150 units x ₹5.80 = **₹870**
* **Total Energy Charge** = ₹350 + ₹870 = **₹1,220** (Not 250 x ₹5.80 = ₹1,450!).

---

### Step 3: Fixed Charges (Sanctioned Load)
Every consumer pays a monthly fixed charge based on their **sanctioned / connected load (in kW)**, regardless of whether you consume any power.
* Standard domestic load in apartments: **2 kW to 5 kW**.
* Typical charge: **₹50 to ₹150 per kW per month**.
* If your connected load is 3 kW at ₹80/kW, your fixed charge is **₹240**.

---

### Step 4: Fuel Surcharge (FAC / FPPCA) and State Electricity Duty
1. **FPPCA / FAC (Fuel Price Adjustment):** Coal and gas price fluctuations incurred by power generation stations are passed on as a variable surcharge (typically **₹0.20 to ₹0.85 per unit**).
2. **Electricity Duty (ED):** State government tax, either a flat rate per unit (e.g., ₹0.15/unit) or a percentage of the energy bill (typically **5% to 16%** depending on state legislation).

## Real Calculation Walkthrough: Delhi vs Maharashtra for 280 Units

Let’s calculate the bill for a household consuming **280 units in a 30-day month** with a **2 kW sanctioned load**:

### Scenario A: Delhi Consumer (with Opted Subsidy)
1. **Units Consumed:** 280 units (falls in 201–400 bracket).
2. **Gross Energy Charge:**
   * First 200 units x ₹3.00 = ₹600
   * Next 80 units x ₹4.50 = ₹360
   * Energy subtotal = ₹960
3. **Fixed Charge (2 kW):** ₹40 x 2 = ₹80
4. **Gross Bill before subsidy:** ₹960 + ₹80 = ₹1,040
5. **Delhi 50% Subsidy Deduction:** -₹520
6. **Total Payable:** **≈ ₹520 + taxes (Under ₹600 total)**.

### Scenario B: Maharashtra Consumer (MSEDCL)
1. **Units Consumed:** 280 units.
2. **Energy Charges:**
   * First 100 units x ₹4.71 = ₹471
   * Next 180 units x ₹10.29 = ₹1,852.20
   * Energy subtotal = ₹2,323.20
3. **Fixed Charge:** ₹128
4. **FAC (₹0.35/unit):** 280 x 0.35 = ₹98
5. **Electricity Duty (16%):** ≈ ₹400
6. **Total Payable:** **≈ ₹2,950 to ₹3,050**.

*Notice how the same 280 units cost ₹600 in Delhi due to subsidies, but ~₹3,000 in Maharashtra due to higher base tariffs and duties!*

## How Solar Rooftop Net Metering Affects Your Bill

If you have installed a grid-tied rooftop solar system (under PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana), your meter is replaced with a **bi-directional Net Meter**:

> **Net Billed Units = Imported Units (from Grid) - Exported Units (from Solar)**

* If your home imported **350 units** from the grid at night, but your solar rooftop exported **400 units** during the day, your **Net Units Consumed = 0**.
* You only pay the minimum fixed demand charges (or receive a credit balance roll-over to the next month).

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### How do I check if my electricity meter is running too fast?
Turn off all main circuit breakers and appliances inside your home for 15 minutes. Check if the red pulsing LED light on your meter continues to blink. If the meter logs units while all appliances are switched off, there is either wiring leakage or a faulty meter requiring DISCOM inspection.

### What is the difference between kW and kWh on the electricity meter?
`kW` (Kilowatt) represents **Power / Demand** (how much electricity you are drawing at that exact second). `kWh` (Kilowatt-hour) represents **Total Energy Consumed** over time (units). You are billed primarily on cumulative `kWh`.

### Is the Delhi 200 unit free electricity subsidy automatic?
No. In Delhi, consumers must submit an electricity subsidy opt-in form (via WhatsApp or the DISCOM web portal) once per year to receive the 100% waiver up to 200 units or 50% subsidy up to 400 units.

### Why is my first electricity bill after moving into a new house so high?
New connections often include non-recurring security deposits, meter installation fees, and an initial full-cycle fixed charge before telescopic usage stabilizes.
