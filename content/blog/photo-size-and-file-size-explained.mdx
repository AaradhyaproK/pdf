Application notifications frequently include technical requirement specifications such as:

> *"Photograph must be 3.5 cm × 4.5 cm, 200 to 300 DPI, between 20 KB and 50 KB in JPG format."*

For many applicants, this combination of physical measurements (centimeters), digital resolution (pixels and DPI), and digital storage weight (kilobytes) is confusing. Why can a 3.5 cm photo be 5 megabytes? And why does resizing the dimensions affect the file size?

This guide breaks down each technical parameter simply and clearly so you never encounter photo upload errors again.

---

## 1. Physical Dimensions (Centimeters / Inches)

Physical dimensions indicate how large the printed image will appear on a paper admit card or identity badge.
- Standard passport photos in India and the UK measure **3.5 cm wide by 4.5 cm high**.
- In the United States, standard passport photos measure **2 inches × 2 inches** (5.1 cm × 5.1 cm).

Because computer screens do not measure in centimeters, these physical dimensions are converted into digital pixels using **DPI (Dots Per Inch)**.

---

## 2. DPI & Pixel Resolution (Width × Height)

**DPI** specifies how many dots of ink or pixels fit into a single inch (1 inch = 2.54 cm).
- At **300 DPI** (standard print quality):
  - 3.5 cm ≈ 1.378 inches × 300 DPI ≈ 413 pixels
  - 4.5 cm ≈ 1.772 inches × 300 DPI ≈ 531 pixels
  - Standard passport dimensions: **413 × 531 pixels**.
- At **200 DPI**:
  - Dimensions equal approximately **275 × 354 pixels**.

---

## 3. Digital File Size (KB vs MB)

Digital file weight measures how much data the image occupies on a hard drive or server:
- 1 Megabyte (MB) = 1024 Kilobytes (KB)
- Modern smartphone cameras shoot pictures at 12 to 48 megapixels, producing files between **3 MB and 10 MB** (3,000 KB to 10,000 KB).
- When a government portal asks for **20KB to 50KB**, you must reduce the file's digital weight by over **98%**.

---

## How Dimensions and File Size Interact

File size is governed by two key variables:
```
File Size ≈ (Pixel Width × Pixel Height) × Compression Quality
```

1. **Downscaling Dimensions:** Reducing an image from 4000 × 3000 pixels (12 megapixels) to 413 × 531 pixels (0.2 megapixels) cuts 98% of the data immediately before any quality reduction.
2. **Adjusting Compression:** Fine-tuning the JPEG compression factor allows you to land precisely at 35KB or 45KB without visible pixelation.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### If I reduce pixel dimensions, will file size decrease automatically?
Yes. Decreasing pixel width and height removes redundant pixel information, which dramatically shrinks file size in KB even before adjusting compression sliders.

### What is the difference between 200 DPI and 300 DPI for online uploads?
For purely digital on-screen display, DPI is just metadata; the actual pixel dimensions (width and height) dictate how the image renders. However, setting 300 DPI ensures crisp printing on physical exam admit cards.
