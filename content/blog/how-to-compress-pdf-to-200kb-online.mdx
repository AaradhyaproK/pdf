## Why Do Scanned PDF Files Exceed 200KB?

Before compressing, understanding why your original PDF is several megabytes helps you get the crispest output possible. 

A multi-page certificate or mark sheet usually inflates in size due to three specific factors:

1. **Excessive Scanner DPI (Dots Per Inch):** Flatbed scanners and office xerox machines typically scan at **300 to 600 DPI in 24-bit TrueColor RGB**. A single A4 page scanned at 600 DPI uncompressed is over 35MB! Even when saved as a PDF, it often retains 3MB to 8MB of redundant pixel data that human eyes cannot distinguish on mobile or computer screens.
2. **Uncompressed Embedded Fonts & Color Profiles:** Many word processors embed complete Unicode font files (including characters in languages you never even typed) along with high-overhead ICC color profiles.
3. **Hidden Metadata & Thumbnail Streams:** Scanners inject metadata headers, camera EXIF information, and thumbnail previews for every page, adding needless kilobytes to the binary stream.

For official government submissions, document portals only require **150 DPI greyscale or balanced color**, which renders text crisp at 100% zoom while easily fitting inside 150KB to 200KB per document.

## 4 Pro Tips: Keeping Text Razor-Sharp Below 200KB

If you have an especially tricky document (like a scanned handwritten certificate), apply these four proven tactics:

1. **Convert to High-Contrast Grayscale First:** Color scanner data takes up 3x more bytes than grayscale. By choosing grayscale or black & white optimization in the compressor, you can retain ultra-sharp 300 DPI text while slashing the file size by 75%.
2. **Crop Redundant Margins:** Scanned files often feature large white margins or black scan bed borders. Using our [Image Cropper](/image/crop) or [PDF Page Organizer](/pdf/organize) to trim unused borders reduces raw pixel dimensions significantly.
3. **Merge Multi-Page Certificates Correctly:** If you scanned front and back sides as separate PDFs, combine them with our [Merge PDF Files Tool](/pdf/merge) *before* compressing so the global font tables and resource dictionaries are unified into a single lightweight file.
4. **Inspect with 100% Zoom:** Always open your downloaded PDF in Adobe Acrobat or Chrome and zoom to 100%. If text remains distinct without stair-stepping pixelation, the recruitment portal's verification officer will approve it without issues.
