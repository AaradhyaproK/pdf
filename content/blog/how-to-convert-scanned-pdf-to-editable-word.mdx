## Why Most PDF to Word Converters Destroy Your Formatting

To understand why tables break and fonts get scrambled during conversion, it helps to know how PDF files actually work under the hood.

A PDF (governed by the **ISO 32000** document exchange standard) does not store content like a word processor. Instead of paragraphs and sentences, a PDF treats every character as a fixed visual coordinate on an X-Y canvas. When an average online converter attempts to build a Word document from this, it makes wild guesses about where tables begin and sentences end.

Scanned documents add another layer of complexity:
- **Flat Image Layer**: Scanned PDFs are simply high-resolution photos (JPEG or TIFF streams) packaged inside a PDF wrapper. Without Optical Character Recognition (OCR), there is no digital text to extract.
- **Skew and Perspective Tilt**: Mobile phone camera captures often have a slight 2-degree tilt or shadows, which fools primitive OCR engines into dropping punctuation.
- **Font Misalignment**: Custom corporate typography gets replaced by default fonts, throwing page breaks and headers completely off balance.

The solution is using a modern client-side document reconstruction engine that analyzes text clusters, aligns tabular grids, and synthesizes clean [OpenXML DOCX](https://www.iso.org/standard/71691.html) streams.
