## What is HEIC and Why Does Apple Use It?

Starting with iOS 11 and macOS High Sierra, Apple adopted **HEIF (High Efficiency Image Format)**—saved with the `.heic` extension—as the default camera capture format instead of standard JPEG.

HEIC utilizes advanced **HEVC (H.265)** compression algorithms originally developed for 4K video. It provides two distinct advantages for smartphone storage:
- **50% Smaller File Size:** An iPhone photo that consumes 4.5 MB in legacy JPEG format takes only 1.8 MB to 2.2 MB in HEIC at identical visual fidelity.
- **Enhanced Color Depth:** While legacy JPEG is limited to 8-bit color (16.7 million colors), HEIC supports **10-bit and 12-bit color channels**, capturing richer highlights, deeper dynamic range, and burst sequences.

### Why Do Job Portals and Windows Reject HEIC Files?

Despite Apple’s technological superiority, 95% of enterprise software, government verification pipelines, and legacy operating systems cannot parse HEIC files for two major reasons:

1. **Strict Licensing & Royalties:** The MPEG-LA consortium charges commercial software vendors hefty licensing fees to embed native HEVC decoders into their servers. Government portals refuse to pay proprietary royalty licenses for image decoders.
2. **Automated Facial Recognition Pipelines:** Portal backends use open-source image libraries (like OpenCV or LibJPEG) that strictly parse standard 8-bit RGB JPEG JFIF streams to detect face dimensions, eye alignment, and signature strokes.

1. **Fix Image Auto-Rotation:** One common flaw of third-party converters is that vertical iPhone portraits turn sideways upon conversion. FileZenith automatically parses the internal EXIF orientation tag (Tag `0x0112`) so your photos remain upright without manual rotating.
2. **Prevent iPhone from Saving HEIC Going Forward:** If you submit forms regularly, you can configure your iPhone camera to shoot in universal JPEG:
   - Go to **Settings > Camera > Formats**.
   - Select **"Most Compatible"** instead of "High Efficiency". All future camera shots will save natively as `.jpg`.
3. **Beware of Blurry Cloud Screenshots:** Many users resort to taking a screenshot of an iPhone photo to bypass HEIC. However, screenshots capture screen resolution (72 DPI) rather than raw camera resolution (300+ DPI), resulting in fuzzy, illegible signatures. Always use an authentic conversion engine instead.
4. **Inspect File Size Limits After Conversion:** While HEIC files are compact, converted JPG files may be 1.5x larger. Always check whether your converted JPG exceeds the portal limit (e.g. 50KB for SSC or 300KB for UPSC).
