## Why Are PDF Files So Big? (The Anatomy of File Bloat)

To understand how to compress a PDF down to 1MB effectively, you need to understand what makes a PDF heavy in the first place:

1. **Uncompressed Embedded Images (DPI Bloat):** When documents are scanned or exported from Canva, images are often embedded at 300 to 600 DPI (dots per inch). While 300 DPI is required for commercial offset printing, computer screens and smartphone displays only require **72 to 150 DPI**.
2. **Redundant Embedded Fonts:** Many PDF editors embed complete font families (including bold, italic, and foreign glyph subsets) multiple times across the document tree instead of referencing standard system fonts.
3. **Hidden Revision History & Metadata:** Modern PDF editing software stores undo states, color profiles, thumbnails, and XML metadata streams that silently add megabytes of invisible weight.
4. **Duplicate Object Streams:** Multi-page documents frequently replicate identical header logos and background graphics on every single page instead of reusing a shared XObject reference.

By applying target-driven compression through the [FileZenith Compress PDF to 1MB Tool](/pdf/compress-to-1mb), unnecessary metadata is stripped, font subsets are deduplicated, and image streams are intelligently resampled to hit the exact target size without sacrificing typographic clarity.
