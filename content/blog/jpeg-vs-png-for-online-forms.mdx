When uploading passport photographs, signature crops, and scanned certificates to online application forms, selecting the incorrect file format (JPEG vs PNG) can lead to portal errors such as *"File format not supported"* or *"File size exceeds maximum threshold"*.

While both JPEG and PNG are universally recognized image formats across the modern web, they were created for fundamentally different use cases.

This guide breaks down the technical differences between JPEG and PNG, explaining why government portals favor JPEG and how to choose the right format for your documents.

---

## Technical Comparison: JPEG vs PNG

| Feature | JPEG (.jpg / .jpeg) | PNG (.png) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Compression Type** | Lossy (Discrete Cosine Transform) | Lossless (DEFLATE algorithm) |
| **File Weight** | Extremely Small (15KB–60KB) | Significantly Larger (200KB–3MB) |
| **Color Support** | 24-bit Truecolor (16.7M colors) | 24-bit RGB + 8-bit Alpha Channel |
| **Transparency** | Not Supported (Solid Background) | Supported (Transparent Background) |
| **Portal Compatibility** | Accepted by 99.9% of forms | Often Rejected or Size-Exceeded |
| **Best For** | Photos, Portraits, Signatures | Logos, Icons, Crisp Vector Graphics |

---

## Why 99% of Online Forms Mandate JPEG

1. **Storage & Bandwidth Constraints:** Millions of candidates upload photos during competitive exam cycles. A 35KB JPEG consumes only a fraction of the storage required by a 500KB PNG.
2. **Predictable File Sizing:** With JPEG, compression quality can be dialed from 0.01 to 1.00, allowing an algorithm to target an exact file size (such as 20KB or 50KB) with mathematical precision.
3. **No Alpha Channel Complications:** Transparent PNG backgrounds often render as pitch black on older government servers that lack alpha channel composite handlers.

---

## When Should You Use PNG?

PNG is the ideal choice for documents requiring lossless, pixel-perfect reproduction, such as:
- Vector logos and digital stamps
- High-contrast line diagrams
- Documents where transparency is required for overlaying on existing backgrounds

However, if you are submitting to an exam portal, banking site, or visa system, **convert your PNG to JPEG** before attempting upload.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Renaming File Extensions:** Simply changing `.png` to `.jpg` in the file name does not convert the internal image format. Most government and university portals have strict MIME validation configured to accept only `image/jpeg` or `image/jpg`. In addition, PNG files easily exceed the 50KB or 20KB file size limits.
